Chapter 1: Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the composition, structure, properties and changes of matter.

Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. It can commonly exist as solid, liquid or gas.

Atomic Mass

Atomic mass represents the relative mass of an atom compared with a standard reference.

Mole Concept

A mole is the amount of substance containing approximately 6.022 × 10²³ particles.

1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Number of moles = Given mass / Molar mass
Example:

Find the number of moles present in 18 g of water.

Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol

Moles = 18 / 18
= 1 mole

Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. Its unit is generally g/mol.

⭐ Mole concept is very important for numerical problems involving chemical equations and stoichiometry.

Chapter 2: Structure of Atom

Subatomic Particles

Electron → negative charge
Proton → positive charge
Neutron → no charge

Atomic Number

Atomic number is the number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom.

Atomic Number (Z) = Number of Protons

Mass Number

Mass Number (A) = Number of Protons + Number of Neutrons
Example:

An atom contains 11 protons and 12 neutrons.

Atomic number = 11
Mass number = 11 + 12
= 23

Electronic Configuration

Electrons are arranged in different shells around the nucleus. The shells are commonly represented by K, L, M, N...

⭐ The electronic configuration helps us understand the chemical behaviour and valency of an element.

Chapter 3: Classification of Elements and Periodicity

Periodic Table

The periodic table arranges elements according to their atomic numbers and shows periodic trends in their properties.

Periods and Groups

Horizontal rows are called periods, while vertical columns are called groups.

Atomic Radius

Atomic radius is a measure of the size of an atom. It generally decreases across a period and increases down a group.

Ionization Energy

Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself in a chemical bond.

⭐ Learn periodic trends carefully because they are frequently useful in comparison-based questions.

Chapter 4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

Chemical Bond

A chemical bond is the attractive force that holds atoms or ions together in a molecule or compound.

Octet Rule

According to the octet concept, atoms tend to achieve a stable arrangement of electrons resembling a noble gas.

Ionic Bond

An ionic bond is formed through transfer of electrons and electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Example:

Sodium loses one electron:
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Chlorine gains one electron:
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Na⁺ and Cl⁻ combine to form NaCl.

Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed by sharing electron pairs between atoms.

H₂ → H—H

Cl₂ → Cl—Cl

Hydrogen Bond

Hydrogen bonding is an attractive interaction involving hydrogen attached to a highly electronegative atom and another electronegative atom.

Chapter 5: Chemical Thermodynamics

System and Surroundings

The part of the universe selected for study is called the system. Everything outside it is called the surroundings.

Internal Energy

Internal energy is the total energy contained within a system.

ΔU = q + w

Here ΔU is change in internal energy, q is heat supplied and w represents work.

Enthalpy

Enthalpy is a thermodynamic quantity used to describe the heat change of a system under constant pressure.

ΔH = H₂ − H₁
⭐ Keep the sign convention clear while solving thermodynamics numerical problems.

Chapter 6: Equilibrium

Meaning of Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state in which the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates.

Equilibrium Constant

For a general reaction:

aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed by changing conditions, the system shifts in a direction that tends to oppose the change.

Example: Changing concentration, pressure or temperature can affect the position of equilibrium.

Chapter 7: Redox Reactions

Oxidation

Oxidation can involve loss of electrons or an increase in oxidation number.

Reduction

Reduction can involve gain of electrons or a decrease in oxidation number.

Oxidation → Loss of electrons

Reduction → Gain of electrons
Example:

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

Zinc loses electrons, so zinc undergoes oxidation.
⭐ Oxidation and reduction occur together in a redox reaction.

Chapter 8: Organic Chemistry – Basic Principles

What is Organic Chemistry?

Organic chemistry mainly deals with carbon compounds.

Tetravalency of Carbon

Carbon has four valence electrons and generally forms four covalent bonds.

Example:

Methane = CH₄
Carbon forms four bonds with four hydrogen atoms.

Structural Representation

Organic compounds can be represented using structural formulas, condensed formulas and other standard representations.

Isomerism

Isomerism occurs when compounds have the same molecular formula but different arrangements or structures.

Chapter 9: Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons containing only single bonds.

General formula of alkanes:
CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond.

General formula:
CₙH₂ₙ

Alkynes

Alkynes contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond.

General formula:
CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
Examples:

Methane → CH₄
Ethene → C₂H₄
Ethyne → C₂H₂
