Chapter 1: Units and Measurements

Physical Quantity

A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured and expressed by a numerical value along with a unit.

Example: 5 metre, 10 kg, 20 second

SI Base Units

The International System of Units (SI) uses seven base units.

Length → metre (m)
Mass → kilogram (kg)
Time → second (s)
Electric current → ampere (A)
Temperature → kelvin (K)

Dimensions

Dimensions represent a physical quantity in terms of fundamental quantities such as mass, length and time.

Velocity = [LT⁻¹]

Acceleration = [LT⁻²]

Force = [MLT⁻²]
⭐ Dimensions are useful for checking whether a physical equation is dimensionally correct.

Chapter 2: Motion in a Straight Line

Distance and Displacement

Distance is the total path travelled by an object. It is a scalar quantity.

Displacement is the shortest distance from the initial position to the final position in a particular direction. It is a vector quantity.

Speed

Speed = Distance / Time

Velocity

Velocity = Displacement / Time

Acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

Here u is initial velocity, v is final velocity and t is time.

Equations of Motion

v = u + at

s = ut + ½at²

v² = u² + 2as
Solved Example:

An object starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity.

u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s

v = u + at
v = 0 + (2 × 5)
v = 10 m/s

Chapter 3: Motion in a Plane

Scalar Quantity

A scalar quantity has only magnitude.

Examples: mass, time, temperature, distance and speed.

Vector Quantity

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Examples: displacement, velocity, acceleration and force.

Vector Components

A vector can be resolved into components along different axes.

Aₓ = A cos θ

Aᵧ = A sin θ

Resultant of Two Perpendicular Vectors

R = √(A² + B²)
If A = 3 units and B = 4 units:

R = √(3² + 4²)
= √25
R = 5 units

Chapter 4: Laws of Motion

Newton's First Law

An object remains at rest or continues in uniform motion in a straight line unless an external unbalanced force acts on it.

Newton's Second Law

The rate of change of momentum of an object is proportional to the applied force.

F = ma

Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Example: When a person pushes a wall, the wall exerts an equal and opposite force on the person.

Momentum

p = mv

where m is mass and v is velocity.

⭐ Remember: Force changes the state of motion of an object.

Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power

Work

Work is done when a force causes displacement of an object.

W = F s cos θ

Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

K.E. = ½mv²

Potential Energy

Gravitational potential energy depends on the height of an object.

P.E. = mgh

Power

Power = Work / Time
Example:

If 100 J of work is done in 5 seconds:

Power = 100 / 5
Power = 20 W

Chapter 6: Gravitation

Universal Law of Gravitation

Every two masses in the universe attract each other with a force that depends on their masses and the distance between them.

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²

Here G is the universal gravitational constant.

Acceleration Due to Gravity

g = GM / R²

Weight

W = mg
Example:

If mass of an object is 10 kg and g = 9.8 m/s²:

W = mg
= 10 × 9.8
W = 98 N

Chapter 7: Thermodynamics

Temperature

Temperature represents the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

Heat

Heat is energy transferred from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature.

First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

Where ΔQ is heat supplied, ΔU is change in internal energy and ΔW is work done by the system.

⭐ The first law expresses the conservation of energy for thermodynamic systems.
