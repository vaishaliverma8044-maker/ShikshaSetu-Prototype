1. Electric Charges and Fields

Electric Charge

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical interaction.

  • Like charges repel each other.
  • Unlike charges attract each other.
  • Charge is conserved.
  • Charge is quantized.

Coulomb's Law

F = k q₁q₂ / r²

where k = 1/(4πε₀).

Electric Field

E = F/q

Electric Field Due to Point Charge

E = kQ/r²

Electric Flux

Φ = E A cosθ

Gauss's Law

Φ = q/ε₀
Exam Tip: Coulomb's law, electric field, electric flux aur Gauss's law ke formulas clearly yaad rakho.

2. Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Electric Potential

V = W/q

Potential Due to Point Charge

V = kQ/r

Potential Energy

U = kq₁q₂/r

Capacitance

C = Q/V

Parallel Plate Capacitor

C = ε₀A/d

With dielectric:

C = Kε₀A/d

Energy Stored in Capacitor

U = ½CV²
Remember: Capacitors in parallel have same potential difference; capacitors in series carry the same charge.

3. Current Electricity

Electric Current

I = Q/t

Ohm's Law

V = IR

Resistance

R = ρL/A

Electrical Power

P = VI
P = I²R
P = V²/R

Electrical Energy

W = VIt

Kirchhoff's Laws

  • Junction rule: Sum of currents entering a junction equals sum leaving it.
  • Loop rule: Algebraic sum of potential changes around a closed loop is zero.

Cell with Internal Resistance

V = E - Ir

4. Moving Charges and Magnetism

Magnetic Force on Moving Charge

F = qvB sinθ

Force on Current Carrying Conductor

F = BIL sinθ

Biot-Savart Law

It gives the magnetic field produced by a current-carrying element.

Magnetic Field Near Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

Force Between Parallel Current Carrying Wires

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂ / 2πd

Torque on Current Loop

τ = NIAB sinθ
Important: Direction questions mein Fleming's left-hand rule / right-hand rule carefully use karo.

5. Magnetism and Matter

Magnetic Dipole

A current loop behaves like a magnetic dipole.

Magnetic Moment = IA

Magnetic Field

Magnetic field is the region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic effects can be observed.

Types of Magnetic Materials

  • Diamagnetic
  • Paramagnetic
  • Ferromagnetic
Remember: Ferromagnetic materials show strong attraction towards magnetic fields.

6. Electromagnetic Induction

Magnetic Flux

Φ = BA cosθ

Faraday's Law

ε = -dΦ/dt

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

Lenz's Law

The induced current acts in a direction that opposes the change producing it.

Motional EMF

ε = Blv
Exam Tip: Faraday's law + Lenz's law se conceptual questions frequently ban sakte hain.

7. Alternating Current

AC Voltage

V = V₀ sinωt

RMS Value

Vᵣₘₛ = V₀/√2
Iᵣₘₛ = I₀/√2

Inductive Reactance

Xᴸ = ωL

Capacitive Reactance

Xᶜ = 1/ωC

LCR Circuit

Z = √[R² + (Xᴸ-Xᶜ)²]

Power in AC

P = Vᵣₘₛ Iᵣₘₛ cosφ

Transformer

Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ
