📐 Mathematics Revision

1. Relations and Functions

Function: A relation in which every element of the domain has exactly one image in the codomain.

One-One: Different elements have different images.

Onto: Every element of codomain has a pre-image.

Bijective: Both one-one and onto.

2. Inverse Trigonometric Functions

sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2
tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2
sin⁻¹(sin x) = x

Inverse trigonometric functions have restricted principal values.

3. Matrices

Order: m × n matrix has m rows and n columns.

A + B = B + A
(AB)C = A(BC)

Matrix multiplication is generally not commutative: AB ≠ BA

Inverse exists when: |A| ≠ 0

4. Determinants

|a b| |c d| = ad − bc

For a square matrix, determinant is a scalar value.

A⁻¹ = adj(A) / |A|

5. Continuity and Differentiability

A function is continuous at x = a if:

lim x→a f(x) = f(a)

Important derivatives:

d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹
d/dx(sin x) = cos x
d/dx(cos x) = −sin x
d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ
d/dx(log x) = 1/x

6. Applications of Derivatives

For increasing function:

f'(x) > 0

For decreasing function:

f'(x) < 0

At a critical point:

f'(x) = 0

7. Integrals

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C
∫1/x dx = log|x| + C
∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C
∫sin x dx = −cos x + C
∫cos x dx = sin x + C

8. Differential Equations

An equation involving derivatives of a dependent variable is called a differential equation.

dy/dx = f(x)

Integrate both sides to obtain the solution.

9. Vector Algebra

a · b = |a||b|cosθ
|a × b| = |a||b|sinθ

Dot product gives a scalar quantity. Cross product gives a vector quantity.

10. Three Dimensional Geometry

Distance = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² + (z₂−z₁)²]

Direction cosines satisfy:

l² + m² + n² = 1

11. Linear Programming

Important terms:

  • Objective function
  • Constraints
  • Feasible region
  • Optimal solution

Maximum or minimum value is generally checked at the corner points of the feasible region.

12. Probability

P(A) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total outcomes
P(A') = 1 − P(A)
P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)
P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B)

⚡ Physics Revision

1. Electric Charges and Fields

F = k q₁q₂/r²
E = F/q
E = kq/r²

SI unit of charge: Coulomb

SI unit of electric field: N/C

2. Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

V = W/q
C = Q/V
Energy = 1/2 CV²

SI unit of capacitance: Farad

3. Current Electricity

V = IR
R = ρL/A
P = VI = I²R = V²/R

SI unit of current: Ampere

SI unit of resistance: Ohm

4. Moving Charges and Magnetism

F = qvB sinθ
F = BIL sinθ

A moving charge/current-carrying conductor can experience magnetic force in a magnetic field.

5. Magnetism and Matter

Magnetic field is represented by magnetic field lines.

Magnetic field lines form closed curves and do not intersect each other.

6. Electromagnetic Induction

ε = −dΦ/dt

Changing magnetic flux produces induced emf.

Principle: Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

7. Alternating Current

V = V₀ sinωt
I = I₀ sinωt
Vᵣₘₛ = V₀/√2

Transformer works on the principle of mutual induction.

8. Electromagnetic Waves

c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)

Electromagnetic waves can travel through vacuum and do not require a material medium.

🧪 Chemistry Revision

1. Solutions

Molarity = moles of solute / volume of solution in litres
Molality = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg

Molarity temperature ke saath change ho sakti hai because volume changes. Molality temperature independent hoti hai because mass does not change.

2. Electrochemistry

E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode
ΔG° = −nFE°

Oxidation occurs at anode and reduction occurs at cathode.

3. Chemical Kinetics

Rate: Change in concentration per unit time.

Rate = k[A]ⁿ

Temperature, concentration and catalyst can affect reaction rate.

4. d- and f-Block Elements

Transition elements generally show variable oxidation states, coloured ions and complex formation.

Lanthanides and actinides are called inner transition elements.

5. Coordination Compounds

A coordination compound contains a central metal atom/ion surrounded by ligands.

Ligand: Ion or molecule donating an electron pair to the central metal.

6. Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

Haloalkanes contain halogen atoms attached to an alkyl group.

Common halogens: F, Cl, Br, I

7. Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Alcohol: Contains −OH attached to an alkyl group.

Phenol: −OH directly attached to an aromatic ring.

Ether: R−O−R'

8. Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

Aldehyde: −CHO

Ketone: −CO−

Carboxylic acid: −COOH

9. Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups.

Functional group: −NH₂

10. Biomolecules

Important biomolecules include:

  • Carbohydrates
  • Proteins
  • Lipids
  • Nucleic acids

11. Polymers

A polymer is a large molecule formed by repeated units called monomers.

Example: Polyethene is formed from ethene monomers.

12. Chemistry in Everyday Life

Important areas include medicines, detergents, antiseptics, disinfectants and food preservatives.
